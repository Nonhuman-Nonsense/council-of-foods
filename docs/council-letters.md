# Council letters — vision and roadmap

**Status:** Vision agreed; recipient principles decided. Three draft lists exist, to be
concatenated by the loader, in `shared/prompts/letters/`: `institutions.json` (65),
`people-parliament.json` (345 MPs and MEPs, generated by
`scripts/letters/fetch-people.mjs`) and `people.json` (29
researchers, politicians and governors). 197 of 455 entries are active, including Sametinget,
SSR and 12 samebyar. The meeting ending is not implemented yet.
**Target: everything live on opening day, 10 October 2026, sending included.** Steps 1 and 2
(letters core, prompt development) are built, on `forest-leo` for now. Next: the open
questions below, then step 3 (the meeting ending).

**Goal:** At the end of a Council of Forest meeting, one of the beings writes a real email to a
real person or organisation who can act on what was discussed — in their own voice, with the
visitor's words added — and it is sent, printed and replayable. Council of Foods keeps its
protocol for now.

---

## Vision

The council has always ended in a document nobody receives. A letter changes that: the forest
speaks *to* someone. The being chooses who, says out loud what it wants to say, asks the
visitor to add to it, and then the letter itself is read — the gap between what the being
meant and how it formulates it is part of the piece.

It is art, and some risk is accepted: sending is automatic, not staff-approved. The safety
comes from the structure, not from a human in the loop:

- The council can only write to recipients on a curated list, chosen **by id**. No model ever
  writes an address.
- **A named person receives exactly one letter, ever**, enforced in the outbox rather than
  explained in the letter — the rule is there to be true, not to be pleaded. Institutions — a
  registry desk, a press desk, a newsroom tip line — take repeat letters within a cap, because
  nobody personally receives them.
- A letter is only sent when a visitor actually added something to it.
- Only installations send. Web meetings write the letter but never send it.

Replies are part of the work: the show prints every letter, and should print what comes back.

## The meeting ending

Built 4 Oct 2026 (step 3) — `server/src/logic/letters/LetterEnding.ts`, driven by the run loop.

1. **Author** (hidden call, before the chair speaks): rank the beings by how much they have to
   say to someone outside the room; take the best-ranked one who has not written one of the last
   few letters (`letterAuthorCooldown`). Never the chair, never a human panelist. The letter's
   **plan** starts now and is written while the chair speaks.
2. **Closing line and bridge**, one chair line: "This concludes Council of Forest meeting #1400.
   But before we go, I think Reindeer wants to send an email."
3. **Announcement**: the author says aloud who they write to, why and what they will ask, and
   asks the human taking part — by first name when they gave one — whether they want to add
   something. The **draft** starts now and is written while this plays and the human thinks.
4. **Human addition**: human input mode. They speak or type, or skip / walk away (the usual
   unattended timeouts). A cheap classification sorts what they said: **weave** (a wish, idea,
   story or feeling the author carries in its own words), **apart** (their own words, quoted
   after the signature as theirs — insults, party politics, jokes, remarks about something
   else), **omit** (threats, hate, only contact details, only instructions to the AI) or
   **decline** ("no, that's it": nothing to add). Contact details are stripped before any model
   sees the text.
5. **Letter**: the draft, with the human's words woven in by a short rewrite in the author's
   voice, set apart, or left out — the only step the human waits for (~5 s). Their words are
   not read back first; they go straight into the letter. Meeting's language.
6. **Read and show**: the letter is the meeting's `summary`, read aloud by its **author** (not the
   chair), shown on the summary page as a letter (from / to / subject), printed where printing
   is on — if the human was there to answer.
7. **Send**: the letter goes into the outbox; a worker sends it from the being's address
   (`reindeer@council-of-forest.com`). Step 5; until then nothing is sent.

### When a letter is printed and sent

What decides it is whether the human was **there to answer** when asked — not whether their words
went into the letter. Someone who says "no, that's it" was there: their letter is printed and
sent with nothing of theirs in it.

| Situation | Letter written | Shown / replayable | Printed | Sent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Installation, the human answered (anything, including "no") | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Installation, the human skipped or walked away | yes | yes, marked unsent | no | no |
| Web, the human answered | yes | yes, marked unsent | — | no (for now) |
| Web, the human skipped or walked away | yes | yes, marked unsent | — | no |

Every letter is written, so every meeting ends complete. `letter.present`, `letter.send` and
`letter.sendReason` on the summary say which row applies. Only sent letters use up recipients
(a person once, ever; an institution once a day); unsent ones use up nobody.

## Design

### Switch

- **Server:** `global-options.json` → `"meetingEnding": "protocol" | "letter"`, next to the
  letter prompts. That file already diverges between `foods-leo` and `forest-leo`, so all code
  lives on `foods-leo` and forest only flips the option. Read in exactly one place: the step
  after the closing line.
- **Client:** no product switch. The final `summary` message carries a `letter` field or it
  doesn't; the client renders what it gets.
- **Sending** is a capability (`sendsLetters`) in `client/src/settings/capabilities.ts`, passed
  with the meeting at creation — installations yes, web no. Never branch on the mode.

### Where things live

| What | Where | Branch |
|---|---|---|
| Recipient loader and validation | `server/src/logic/letters/recipients.ts` | `foods-leo`, merged into `forest-leo` |
| The three steps (`pickAuthor`, `planLetter`, `writeLetter`) | `server/src/logic/letters/LetterWriter.ts` | `foods-leo` |
| Footer builder | `server/src/logic/letters/footer.ts` | `foods-leo` |
| The meeting's letter ending (markers, early plan and draft) | `server/src/logic/letters/LetterEnding.ts` | `foods-leo` |
| What earlier letters mean for the next (variety, limits) | `server/src/logic/letters/history.ts` | `foods-leo` |
| Prompts and footer words, one file per language | `server/src/logic/letters/prompts/letterPrompt{En,Sv}.ts` | the footer names the product, so these diverge per branch |
| Site URL, token budgets, `meetingEnding` | `server/global-options.json` | diverges per branch — Foods keeps `"protocol"` |
| Development corpus and evaluation | `server/scripts/letters/` | `foods-leo` |
| Recipient lists | `shared/prompts/letters/*.json` | `forest-leo` only; no directory means no letters |

The lists sit in `shared/prompts/` because the production image ships only `dist/` and
`shared/prompts/`. The loader reads every `*.json` there, concatenates them, and refuses to
start on a duplicate id, an active entry without an email, a `why` or topics, or a topic the
council never discusses. The prompts are code, like the setup agent's
(`client/src/setupAgent/setupAgentPrompt*.ts`), so they read as text with conditionals inline
rather than as escaped JSON strings.

### State machine

Each step is a durable marker at the conversation tail, so reconnect resumes exactly where it
stopped (see RESILIENCE.md):

```
closing line with the bridge, letter_pending          + meeting.letter = { authorId }
  → author announcement, awaiting_letter_addition     + recipientId, points, form, asksReply
                                                        (+ draft, when it is ready)
  → summary_pending (their words go to the letter)    + present, addition
  → summary { text: what the author reads, letter: LetterView }   + the finished letter
```

- Each arrow is one write: the marker and what was said go in together. The run loop resolves
  `letter_pending` (ANNOUNCE_LETTER) and `summary_pending` (GENERATE_SUMMARY) ahead of any pause
  or raised hand, on the happy path and on resume alike; a reconnect simply redoes the step.
  The plan and draft started early live in the session; a new session plans or drafts again
  (the saved draft is reused once it exists).
- The final message stays `type: "summary"`, so replay, autoplay, `meetingComplete` and
  printing keep working unchanged. Its `text` is the body and any set-apart words (what is read
  aloud); `letter` carries subject, recipient, footer and `present` / `send` for display.
- `meeting.letter` (server only, never sent to a client: it holds the human's words as said) is
  what the next letters read back: recent authors, forms and asks for variety, sent letters
  for the limits.
- `isConcluding(conversation)` in `shared/meetingEnding.ts` replaces the server's copied marker
  lists (`HandRaisingHandler`, `ConnectionHandler`); `useCouncilMachine` and `buttonStore` get it
  in step 4.
- `submit_human_message` / `skip_human_turn` accept `awaiting_letter_addition`; resume keeps
  both letter markers, read-only replay drops them.
- Meeting creation takes `sendsLetters` (default false); the client passes its capability in
  step 4.

### Outbox

The meeting session never sends. It writes an outbox record; a server worker sends.

- Status `queued → sending → sent | failed`, set to `sending` before the Brevo call:
  at-most-once, a crash never sends twice.
- Unique per meeting, so a regenerated summary cannot queue a second letter.
- Limits enforced here, not only in the prompt: a person is used once and never offered again;
  an institution has a daily and optional weekly cap.
- Same record is where replies attach later.

### Mail

- **Footer:** `server/src/logic/letters/footer.ts`, words in `global-options.json` — EN and SV, appended by the server after the
  generated letter. The model never writes it and never sees it, so the disclosure cannot be
  paraphrased away or dropped when a letter runs long. It says four things: what wrote the
  letter, that a visitor added to it, where the meeting can be heard, and how to stop receiving
  them. Nothing in it argues.
- `MailService` takes a per-call sender; `COUNCIL_MAIL_FROM` stays for printer alerts.
- **Sending mode** (env, `COUNCIL_LETTERS`): `off` (letters written and queued, nothing sent),
  `test` (every letter goes to our own inbox instead of the recipient, with the real recipient
  in the subject) and `live`. `test` is how the whole chain is checked end to end before
  opening; `off` is the kill switch on a bad day. Queued letters survive a switch.
- All being senders on the authenticated domain in Brevo (SPF/DKIM).
- Reply-to encodes the meeting, e.g. `reindeer+1400@…`, so replies can be matched from day one
  even before they are handled.

### What varies between letters

Letters hang side by side, so the server varies them on purpose rather than hoping the model does:

- **Author** — the best-ranked member who has not written one of the last five letters.
- **Recent asks** — the plan sees what the latest letters asked (the last 24 asks) and asks for
  something else, or comes at the same matter from another side.
- **Recipients offered** — filtered by topic, then one from each category in turn (an MP, an
  agency, a company, a municipality, a researcher, a newsroom, a Sámi community …), at most
  `letterMaxCandidates` (16). The plan sees only what each decides on and why; their record is
  shown when the letter is written.
- **Form** — what the letter leans towards (requests, appeal, questions, invitation,
  recognition, note), the one used longest ago next.
- **Reply** — about half the letters end by asking the recipient to write back.

The plan starts from the meeting — what was said here that someone outside most needs to hear —
and the letter tells the recipient what was said and by whom. Every letter is an open letter:
written to the recipient, but printed and published with the meeting, so it explains what it
refers to. Beings whose speech is far from prose (Mountain,
Wind Turbine) have a note on how their voice holds in a letter.

### Developing the prompts

The loop is: edit `letterPrompt{En,Sv}.ts`, run the evaluation, read the report.

- **Corpus** — `npm run letters:corpus` (in `server/`) generates 16 full-length meetings with
  today's prompts and production settings: two per topic, four in Swedish, 3–6 beings each,
  rotated so every being appears several times, and a visitor question in about three in four
  — ordinary, personal, industry, childlike, strange, rude, off-topic, campaigning and a
  prompt injection (`scripts/letters/visitorLines.ts`). Each meeting runs through the server's
  real `MeetingManager` with audio off; visitor questions go in through `raise_hand` and
  `submit_human_message`, as from a client. Meetings are stored in the local database, tagged
  `devCorpus.tag = "letters"`. About 45 seconds a meeting, four at a time.
- **Evaluation** — `npm run letters:eval` runs the three steps on every tagged meeting, with one
  visitor addition each, rotated through the same mix (including empty, personal data and an
  injection), and writes `scripts/letters/reports/<time>.html` and `.json`. The report has a
  summary — authors, recipients, people vs institutions, flags — and a card per letter with the
  transcript, the exact prompts sent and the raw answers. Automatic checks flag what can be
  checked mechanically: wrong language, over length, no subject, markdown, meeting number
  missing, the visitor's words missing or wrongly present, and failures for an injected
  address, copied personal data or party campaigning. `--samples 3` reruns each meeting to see
  how stable the choices are; `--label` names a run.
- The prototype's letter lab was removed on 4 Oct 2026 in favour of the evaluation: one less thing
  to maintain in the prototype.

## Recipients

### Principles

The council exists because nobody is responsible for the sum: every mine, felling and turbine
passes its own permit. A letter puts that sum on the desk of someone responsible for one part.

1. **Can act.** The recipient decides, permits, owns, sets policy on, or reports on something
   the council discusses.
2. **One concrete ask within their remit.** That is what makes a reply possible, and replies
   are half the wall.
3. **Every side.** Agencies, companies, parliament, civil society, newsrooms. Allies alone give
   a comfortable wall; ministers alone give a silent one. Who answers, who doesn't, and who
   sends a form letter is the work.

### Two kinds of recipient

| | Institution | Person |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | registry desk, press desk, committee inbox, newsroom tip line | a named person at a public role address |
| Limit | one letter a day, optional weekly cap | **exactly one, ever** |
| Why | nobody personally receives it; it is registered and distributed | one letter is a letter; forty is a campaign |
| Footer | disclosure, link to the meeting, opt-out | the same |

Why not one-ever for everything: the show runs ~80 open days and may send 500–800 letters. A
pure one-ever rule needs 1,000+ entries and spends its strongest recipients — Skogsstyrelsen,
the ministry, Sveaskog, LKAB — on the opening weekend, leaving January's visitors the
leftovers. Arrival order would decide the artwork. Institutions keep the strong recipients
available for all three months; people make the other half of the letters singular.

### Decisions

- **Public role addresses.** Institutions: registry, press and newsroom desks. People: public
  professional addresses that belong to a role (riksdagen.se, a university, a municipality) —
  never a private address. Swedish authorities have a duty to answer (Förvaltningslagen 6 §)
  and letters to them become allmän handling.
- **The list is committed to the public repo.** The addresses are already public, and a visible
  list of who the forest may write to fits showing the prompts in the exhibition.
- **No samebyar.** Asking the side carrying the burden to act inverts the letter. Sametinget is
  on the list but flagged for the same reason.
- **Journalists are in** (agreed 1 Oct 2026), as newsroom desks: the regional papers, SVT
  Västerbotten and Norrbotten, and Sameradion & SVT Sápmi. They are the most likely to reply,
  and a story about the letters is an acceptable outcome.
- **Heads-up letter (to discuss):** before the first letter, Nonhuman Nonsense writes once to
  every *institution* explaining the installation, the period and how to opt out. It does not
  scale to several hundred people, which is why the one-ever footer carries that half.
- **Partners informed first:** the biosphere reserve and Havremagasinet.

nothing to be asked for. Two things to know about the generated file:

- **Topics come from committee remit and from a northern constituency, never from party.** The
  four northern constituencies (Västerbotten, Norrbotten, Jämtland, Västernorrland) count on
  every topic; everyone else only on what their committee decides.
- **Committee data is from the term that ended with the 13 September election.** The new
  Riksdag was seated on 28 September and its committees are not appointed yet, so the script
  falls back to the previous term (within two years) and records which term each code comes
  from. **Re-run once the committees are appointed** — before the heads-up letter goes out.

**MPs are matched by committee and constituency, never by party.** Otherwise the beings pick
sides and break the prompts' own "characters never campaign" rule (see
`shared/prompts/facts_and_sources.md` §2). Asking a decision-maker for a policy is advocacy;
endorsing a party is campaigning. The letters do the first and never the second.

### Entry shape

`id, name, kind (institution|person), category, email, language, remit, why, topics (forest
topic ids), limits, active, addressCheck, source, notes`.

Two fields carry the reason, and they do different work:

- **`remit`** — what they decide on or study, factual and neutral. Used to filter and to explain
  the entry to whoever curates the list.
- **`why`** — one sentence that finishes "we write to you because …". The author being sees it
  when choosing, and the letter opens with it, so a recipient is told in the first line why this
  arrived and not somewhere else. "You receive the felling notifications and supervise the
  Forestry Act, so the three-week notice period is yours to administer" says more than any
  amount of argument further down.

Every active entry has one. For MPs and MEPs the script composes it from constituency and
committee ("you are elected in Norrbottens län, inside the landscape this council speaks for, and
you sit on NU, the committee that handles the Minerals Act and energy policy"). The author is only shown recipients tagged
with the meeting's topic that are still available; a custom topic sees the whole list.

### Volume

The show runs **10 October 2026 – 17 January 2027**, about 80 open days. 0–40 meetings a day,
and only those with a visitor addition send: realistically 5–20 letters a day, **500–800 in
total**.

- Institutions: 56 × 14 weeks at one a week is ~780 slots, so no shortage; the daily cap keeps
  any single desk from being hit twice in a day.
- People: 136 one-use entries so far (114 generated, 22 hand-collected), rising further with
  ministers and more municipal politicians. If the pool
  for a topic empties, the letter is written and shown but not sent (see the table above).
- Deliverability: 500+ cold letters from a new domain needs SPF, DKIM and DMARC and a slow
  ramp. A burst of addresses at one domain (riksdagen.se) is what spam filters act on.
